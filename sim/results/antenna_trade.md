# Payload antenna trade study (illustrative)

Same S-band aperture for NB-IoT uplink and downlink, 550 km. Service column: 24 satellites at 45°, worst South African site (incl. EEZ). Generated by `sim/antenna_trade.py`.

| Option | Uplink from | Downlink from | Usable mask | SA typical wait | SA longest wait | DC power | Mass | Est. cost | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Patch 14 dBi, ±30° beam, 2 W (baseline) | 22° | 37° | **37°** | 32 min | 4.2 h | 12 W | 0.4 kg | $15k | Simple, cheap. Narrow footprint. |
| B. Patch 14 dBi, 10 W PA | 22° | 24° | **24°** | 20 min | 3.3 h | 45 W | 0.7 kg | $40k | Brute force. DC power and thermal hard on 6U. |
| C. Isoflux 5 dBi peak at 20°, 2 W | 10° | never (best -1.4 dB) | **no** | — | — | 12 W | 0.5 kg | $35k | Shaped beam cancels range loss. Even footprint. |
| D. Isoflux 5 dBi peak at 20°, 5 W | 10° | never (best +2.6 dB) | **no** | — | — | 25 W | 0.6 kg | $45k | Isoflux with margin. |
| D2. Isoflux 7 dBi peak at 30°, 5 W | 15° | 21° | **21°** | 16 min | 2.5 h | 25 W | 0.6 kg | $45k | Narrower shaped cone: more gain, smaller footprint. |
| D3. Isoflux 5 dBi peak at 20°, 8 W | 10° | 16° | **16°** | 11 min | 1.6 h | 38 W | 0.7 kg | $55k | Wide shaped beam, enough power to close. |
| E. 4×4 phased array 17 dBi, 4 W total | 20° | 20° | **20°** | 16 min | 2.2 h | 30 W | 1.2 kg | $150k | Steers to users; most capacity. Highest cost/risk. |
| F. Baseline + deeper coverage enhancement | 16° | 26° | **26°** | 21 min | 3.5 h | 12 W | 0.4 kg | $15k | More NB-IoT repetitions: ~4× less throughput per device. |

**Recommendation (illustrative):** fly the demonstrator with the simple patch plus deeper coverage enhancement (F, software only), so devices can attach from ~26°. Design the Stage 2 payload around a shaped isoflux antenna (D2/D3), which gives the lowest mask for its cost if the bus can supply ~25–40 W during passes. Keep the phased array (E) for Stage 4.

Reading the table:
- The baseline patch is limited by the **downlink**: devices hear nothing below ~37°, so they can't attach.
- Raising power alone (B) helps, but costs DC power and heat a 6U can't easily spare.
- A **shaped isoflux beam** (C/D) gives the same margin at every elevation, but a wide beam has little gain. At 2–5 W it sits just below the 3 dB target. It closes with a narrower cone (D2) or ~8 W (D3).
- A phased array (E) gives the most capacity but is the Stage 4+ option, not the demonstrator.
- Deeper repetition (F) is a software lever. It works but cuts throughput per device.

*Illustrative. Replace gains, powers, masses and costs with vendor data before decisions.*
