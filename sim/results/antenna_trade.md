# Payload antenna trade study (illustrative)

The same S-band antenna serves the NB-IoT uplink (receive) and downlink (transmit). A device must hear the downlink before it can send, so the usable mask is the higher of the two. Antenna gain is tied to beam shape: a ±30° patch has about 9.7 dBi and a ±20° patch about 12.9 dBi (80% efficiency); an isoflux antenna shaped out to 20° elevation can have at most about 7.7 dBi at its edge. ## What changed from the first version

- **The patch was too optimistic.** The first version gave a ±30° patch 14 dBi. Physics allows about 9.7 dBi. The baseline patch now closes the uplink from about 29° and the downlink from about 47°, not 22° and 37°.
- **The isoflux figures hold.** 5 dBi at the edge of a 20° cone is inside the physical limit (about 7.7 dBi), so the 16° Stage 3 mask still needs the isoflux antenna with about 8 W of RF power (about 38 W DC during passes).
- **The downlink sets the mask in every NB-IoT option.**

## Recommendation (illustrative)

- **Demonstrator (6U or hosted payload):** simple patch plus deep coverage mode (F). It proves the link and the software. Coverage is poor by design; it is a demonstrator.
- **Stage 2–3 service (12U):** isoflux antenna with 8 W (D3) to hold the 16° mask. Ask bus suppliers to confirm about 38 W for the payload during passes. If the bus cannot supply it, fall back to 5 W with deep coverage mode for edge-of-footprint devices (D4), or a 450 km orbit (G).
- **Keep the compact protocol (H) as the capacity and power hedge.** It closes at 2 W and carries about three times the messages per carrier, but needs our own device firmware.
- **Phased array (E):** Stage 4, when capacity needs several beams.

Check the downlink power against ITU power-flux-density limits for the band before fixing it (see `docs/itu/FILING_PARAMETERS.md`).
