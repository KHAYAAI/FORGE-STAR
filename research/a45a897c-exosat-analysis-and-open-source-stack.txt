2. Everything Required to Build a Similar Business
Building a real competing LEO constellation is one of the hardest things you can attempt in technology. Here is the full list of what is required, broken into major categories.
A. Technical / Engineering Requirements

Satellite design (bus + payload): power systems, propulsion, attitude control, thermal, radiation tolerance, phased-array antennas, software-defined radios.
Ability to design for very low cost while still surviving LEO.
High-volume manufacturing process (not building one-off satellites).
Ground segment: gateways, network operations center, user terminals / integration with existing 3GPP devices.
Constellation management software (collision avoidance, spectrum sharing, traffic routing, software updates).
End-to-end system simulation and testing capabilities.

B. Regulatory & Spectrum (Critical Path Item)

ITU satellite network filing (through a national administration — Singapore, Luxembourg, etc.).
Coordination with other satellite operators (very time-consuming).
National licenses in every country you want to serve (market access).
Frequency assignments for user links (L/S-band for D2C/IoT, Ka/Ku for broadband) and feeder links.
Compliance with ITU milestone requirements (you must launch a percentage of the constellation by certain deadlines or lose the filing).
Export control strategy (the whole point of being ITAR/EAR-free).

C. Manufacturing & Supply Chain

Access to reliable, low-cost component suppliers that have zero U.S.-origin controlled technology.
Ability to manufacture or assemble at scale in friendly jurisdictions.
Quality control and testing facilities (vibration, thermal vacuum, EMC, etc.).
Long-term supplier contracts for critical parts (solar panels, batteries, thrusters, antennas, processors).

D. Launch

Secured launch contracts (rideshares or dedicated).
Ability to launch from non-U.S. providers if you want full ITAR freedom (India, Europe, China, private providers, etc.).
Launch cadence that matches your constellation build-out plan.
Insurance for launches and in-orbit operations.

E. Capital
Realistic capital needs (very approximate):

Prototype / first few satellites + first launch: tens of millions
Initial commercial constellation (dozens to low hundreds of satellites): hundreds of millions
Full mega-constellation: multiple billions

Collision avoidance and space traffic management.

Continuous spectrum monitoring and interference management.

Year 0–1: Small team, raise seed, design & build first prototype satellite, file spectrum, secure first launch slot, sign LOIs.
Year 1–2: Launch 1–few satellites, demonstrate basic service (usually IoT first), raise Series A/B.

Year 4+: Scale to full constellation and broader services (broadband, edge compute, etc.).
